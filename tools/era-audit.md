# Keeping the lore in its own era

This addon covers patch 1.12 and earlier, plus Forever's own content, and
nothing in between or after.

On 2026-11-09 four entries were found describing things that do not exist yet
in that period. This file was written for those four. What it says about them
is still true and is kept below, because the way they got in turned out to be
the way most of the rest got in too.

The four were not the problem. They were the sample. A sweep ran from
2026-11-10 to 2026-11-24 and read every vanilla entry in the addon against the
1.12 client's own data: thirty-seven review runs, with the ledger and the gates
built first. `tools/era-sweep.md` is its working log, run by run, and is the
place to look for any single entry. This file is what the whole of it adds up
to.

## What the sweep did to the corpus

Measured by dumping every entry's prose at the tag before the sweep began and
again at the end, rather than by adding up the runs' own claims:

| | Before | After |
|---|---:|---:|
| Entry records | 994 | 983 |
| Vanilla records | 921 | 907 |
| Forever records | 73 | 76 |

Of the 906 vanilla records that existed both before and after, **692 changed
and 214 were left exactly as they were**. So roughly three entries in four
needed something done to them, and the quarter that did not is the measure of
how much was sound to begin with.

Eleven records left the vanilla corpus. Five were deleted because the place
does not exist in this period at all: **The Sludgewerks**, a Bilgewater base in
neither client; **Whisperwind Grove**, the Emerald Circle's Cataclysm town;
**The Red Reaches**, an area added in 4.0.3a; and **Thieves Camp** and
**Witherbark Caverns**, both planned subzones the wiki marks as removed from
beta. The other six were duplicates of a record that already existed,
resolved in favour of the spelling the client itself uses. Three records were re-tagged from
vanilla to Forever rather than removed, because they are Forever's content and
belong in the corpus judged against Forever's own sources: **Galak Camp**,
**Legashi Stronghold** and **Skywatcher Plateau**.

## The original four

Kept because they are the clearest example of the commonest way in, and because
the gates described below were built to stop exactly these.

| Entry | What it said | What is true at 1.12 |
|---|---|---|
| Southern Barrens | The lower half of the Barrens, "past the Great Divide" | The Great Divide is a lava chasm the Cataclysm opened. In 1.12 the halves are divided by a dried-up riverbed |
| Gold Road | Runs from the Mor'shan Rampart "down to the Great Divide" | It runs the length of the zone, crossing that riverbed on a bridge; south of the bridge it is the Southern Gold Road |
| The Headland | A Stormpike training camp with instructors and a drill sergeant | The Stormpikes arrive in Cataclysm. In 1.12 the ridge holds mountain lions and nothing else |
| Southbreak Shore | Captain Dreadbeard leads the pirates there | Dreadbeard is Cataclysm. In 1.12 it is Southsea Freebooters, with sea giants in the surf |

Two further corrections came out of the same pass. Gavin's Naze named the four
ridges above Hillsbrad as Sofera's Naze, Corrahn's Dagger, Dandred's Fold and
itself; the wiki numbers them and the third is the Headland, not Dandred's
Fold, and the Headland is not named after a person, so the paragraph built on
that observation was wrong twice. Shandris Feathermoon was given the rank of
General, which she does not hold on a 1.12 nameplate.

## The error classes, in order of how much trouble they caused

**1. A later state of the place, described as the present.** The original four,
and the class the sweep was started for. It is the hardest to see by reading,
because every word of it can be accurate about somewhere that exists.

**2. The wrong occupants.** The commonest finding of all, and present in
nearly every run. Vul'Gol Ogre Mound had Vul'Gol ogres pressed by worgen; the
ogres are Splinter Fist under Zzarc' Vul and there are no worgen. Southbreak
Shore had Captain Dreadbeard; it is Southsea Freebooters. Windshear Mine was a
working Horde mine the goblins had left; the Venture Company is still at the
mouth of it and the kobolds in the shafts have an overlord of their own.

**3. The wrong place.** Right name, wrong ground. Baradin Bay was drawn across
half a continent. Zuuldaia Ruins was put a short walk from Booty Bay's docks
and is at the opposite end of Stranglethorn. The Wildmane Water Well was out in
the western plains and is north of Thunder Bluff, which a 1.12 quest says in
words. Chillwind Point was the wrong place entirely.

**4. Invention.** A claim with no source anywhere, usually filling a gap the
sources leave. Two shapes recurred often enough to name: an explanation of how
a place got its name, and a story of agricultural or civic decline. The Dead
Acre and the Dust Plains both grew a failed-farmland narrative, in different
runs, from nothing. Iceflow Lake had a shipwreck under the ice.

**5. A proper noun inferred from a common word in the place name.** Named at
Twilight Vale in run 36 and found twice more immediately after. Twilight Vale
had the Twilight's Hammer holding ground in Darkshore, built out of the
coincidence that a cult two zones away shares the word; the name is the
half-light the coast sits in. Vul'Gol invented a tribe the same way. Whispering
Gardens became a graveyard with warded graves, and Witch Hill filled up with
undead, both read straight off the name. This class is worth naming separately
because the prose it produces is confident, specific and entirely fictional,
and nothing mechanical will ever flag it.

**6. A claim asserted where the source says nobody knows.** Staghelm Point is
named for one of two Staghelms and it is not recorded which. The entry picked
one. Several others did the same thing more quietly.

**7. Lore published long after the period, about events inside it.** The line
drawn here took two runs to settle and matters for anyone adding entries. The
Scarab Wall's builder, from a 2023 book, was refused: a fact being set in the
distant past is not the same as its being known in this period. Madoran
Bronzebeard was accepted on the opposite reasoning, because he is named in the
History of Warcraft books a 1.12 player can read in the game. Woodpaw Den lost
a paragraph sourced to a 2018 novel on the first rule.

**8. Our own entries contradicting each other.** Found repeatedly once enough
of the corpus had been checked to make it visible. Aldrassil contradicted the
Teldrassil account twice in one sentence. North Gate Pass lost an argument with
North Gate Outpost. Wildwind Lake put the Grimtotem in Camp Mojache when the
Camp Mojache entry already had them in their own compound to the west.

**9. The sweep's own corrections, wrong in turn.** This became the single most
reliable source of new findings. Run 7 found run 5's Bristleback error, run 35
found two sentences run 34 had written while fixing something else, and run 37
found a haunting run 34 had put at Witch Hill that nothing in 1.12 supports.
The cause is always the same asymmetry: the claim under test gets the scrutiny,
and the words travelling alongside it ride free.

Run 10 found the other half of it, which is that two of its own four findings
were corrections never carried across. The Maraudon fix and the Isle of Dread
fix had each been applied to the entry the error was spotted in, while the
other entries repeating the same claim were left alone. **A correction is not
finished when the entry it was found in is fixed.** Grep the rest of the corpus
for the claim before closing it out.

## How they got in

The original diagnosis held up across all 907 entries, so it is unchanged:
nearly every error came from a retail wiki article, which covers a place across
every expansion it has existed in, with the current state in the lead and the
earlier states under headings, in galleries or in image captions. Read quickly,
the lead reads like the truth about the place.

The tells, in order of reliability:

- An article at `Foo (Classic)` exists. Then the bare `Foo` is the retail one.
  A `(Classic)` **redirect** settles nothing; only a real article does.
- The infobox image is captioned `Foo (Cataclysm).jpg`, and a gallery lower
  down has `Foo.jpg | In Classic`.
- A `{{Patch X.Y.Z|added=}}` section dates the place exactly, and an earliest
  patch of 2.0.1 or later is out of era.
- Cited quests are Cataclysm quests, and ability ids are five digits starting
  with 7 or 8. A 1.12 spell id is four digits or low five.
- The NPC named in the lead is absent from a 1.12 creature table.

Three further routes in turned up during the sweep that the first pass had not
seen:

- **An infobox leader who is somewhere else.** The wiki files a tribe's leader
  under the tribe's best-known holding, which is not always where the client
  puts him. Jadefire Glen's leader is the clear case, and Tortusk at Thorn Hill
  and Berrin Burnquill at the Tkashi Ruins were both kept out on the same
  grounds, having no 1.12 creature row at all.
- **The worklist keeping a place alive.** `tools/places.tsv` still held a row
  for The Red Reaches after the entry was deleted, so the place was eligible to
  be written up again. A generated file's header did the same thing for
  `tools/area-gaps.tsv`.
- **Absence from a spawn table read as absence from the game.** Magus Rimtori
  has no row in `creature` and is template 8578 with AI scripts and two quests.
  Check templates before concluding a character does not exist.

## What stops it now

**`tools/era-terms.tsv`, the term ledger, gated by `tools/validate.lua`.** The
era check used to be a blacklist of 26 terms somebody had thought of, and a
blacklist can only fail on what is already known to be wrong. That is why
Auberdine kept a boat on "the Azuremyst route north" through several releases:
nothing in the list said Azuremyst, and the route arrives in patch 2.0.1. It is
now a whitelist. Every proper noun the prose uses needs a row carrying a
verdict and the evidence for it, and a proper noun with no row fails the build,
so anything new is rejected until somebody files it.

The ledger stands at 2,480 rows against 8,965 proper nouns taken out of the
prose: 2,327 `vanilla`, 58 `forever`, 45 `out-of-era`, 25 `history` and 25
`fragment`. Entry names and `readMore` are exempt, and only `hook`, `lore` and
`didYouKnow` are read, because an entry may legitimately be called the Southern
Barrens while being forbidden to describe the chasm that is not there yet.
Matching is case sensitive, so "the northern Barrens" is a direction and passes
while "the Northern Barrens" is a zone the Cataclysm made and does not.

**`tools/check-era.py`.** The ledger cannot catch a character who simply does
not exist yet, because nothing about the name gives it away. That needs data.
cmangos/classic-db publishes the 1.12.1 world database, and `creature_template`
is every creature in the game at that patch:

    python3 -I tools/check-era.py --download

It prints a list rather than passing or failing, because absence has two
meanings. A dead king is absent and in scope. A captain standing on a beach is
absent and out of it.

The dump is GPL-3.0 and this addon is MIT, so it is never committed, and
neither is any bulk extract of it or of the wago.tools CSVs: download to a
temporary directory, read it, leave it there. That is also why this check is
not in CI.

## What no gate can catch

Being plain about this, because it is where the next error will come from.

The ledger proves that a name existed. It cannot prove that the sentence around
the name is true. Every one of error classes 2 through 9 above passes the gate
cleanly, because each is built entirely out of words that are vanilla. Twilight
Vale's cult used `Twilight's Hammer`, which is in the ledger and correct, in a
zone where no cult stands. The only thing that catches that class is reading
the place's own sources and asking what is actually there.

Two further limits are worth knowing. A term the extractor never sees is never
checked: a word capitalised only at the start of a sentence is discarded, which
hid `Security Chief Bilgewhizzle` from the gate in two entries until a third
wrote him mid-sentence. And the same rule works in reverse, so making a term
more specific in one entry can turn a common word into a proper noun corpus
wide and fail entries nobody touched. That happened in five consecutive runs.
Adding a row is always safe; deleting or re-verdicting one is not.

## What was checked and came back clean

- Every capitalised phrase used twice or more across all vanilla entries,
  against both the 1.12 creature table and the addon's own place names.
- Every place-shaped name in the prose that the addon does not itself register.
- The 214 vanilla entries the sweep read and left exactly as they were.
- The claims that looked like the same mistake and were not: Baine Bloodhoof
  gives the Bael'dun quest in 1.12 and is at Bloodhoof Village; Honor's Stand is
  a Horde position in 1.12 and only becomes Alliance in Cataclysm; Corrahn's
  Dagger is Syndicate ground before the Stormpikes take it; Araga is a 1.12
  rare; Nat's Landing and the Defiler's Den are both vanilla; Camp Taurajo is
  standing; the Great Lift really is tauren-built; Doomhammer really was run
  down by a wagon; the Deeprun Tram really is partly flooded.

## The rule this leaves

For a vanilla place, read the `(Classic)` article when one exists, check any
character named in the lead against the 1.12 creature table, and prefer the
client's own data to the wiki whenever the two disagree: `creature_template`,
`gameobject_template` and `quest_template` for what is there, `AreaTable` for
what the place is called, the wiki for what neither answers.

For a Forever place, Forever's own article is the source and its characters are
correctly absent from 1.12. Never flag a Forever entry for that absence.

And when a claim cannot be supported from a vanilla source, delete it. Do not
reword it into something vaguer and keep it, and do not replace it with a
second unsourced claim. Error class 9 above is what happens when that rule is
relaxed: a softened sentence reads as though it has been checked, and the next
pass has no way to tell that it has not.
